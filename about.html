About

I consider myself a researcher first. I am currently studying neuroscience independently and hope to pursue a formal education in the field. When a question from neuroscience or psychology interests me, I often try to turn it into an application, a tool, or a working model.

I use AI as the main implementation and analysis layer in my projects. Models write most of the code and help with research, review, and technical options. I set the problem and direction, inspect the result, and decide what is worth keeping and where the project should go next. In areas where I do not know enough, I sometimes delegate part of the technical decision-making as well.

Many of my applications start with a problem I run into myself. Sometimes an existing tool adds too many unnecessary steps, demands more attention than it should, or simply does not fit how I use it. If the feature I need is missing or the tool gets in the way more than it helps, I try to make my own version. First for myself, then for people with a similar problem.

I do not think a product has to be for everyone. If it solves a concrete problem well for a small group of people, that is already enough.

I am most interested in qualia and subjective experience, the possibility of consciousness in artificial systems, mathematical models of neurobiological processes that can be implemented in code, and how the brain encodes and decodes information. I am also interested in statistics and AI/ML.

My projects usually begin as fairly large ideas. Once I start working on them, it becomes clearer what can actually be built, which directions are worth testing, and what is realistic enough to finish. So far I have mostly worked independently, but I would like experience working in a team, especially with people who understand AI/ML, think independently, and can approach a familiar problem from a different angle.

If one of the projects interests you, you have criticism or feedback, or you want to discuss working together, send me an email.
